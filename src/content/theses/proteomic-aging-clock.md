Proteins are the molecular workhorses of life, responsible for nearly every function within living
cells. Understanding how protein expression changes over time can reveal critical insights into
health, aging, and disease. With the help of tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS), scientists can now
identify and quantify thousands of proteins in biological samples, making it possible to track how
protein profiles evolve in response to aging and disease.

Recent advances have revealed that protein abundance patterns can serve as a kind of molecular
"aging clock" — one that shows strong associations with the onset of chronic diseases (cardiovascular,
metabolic, neurodegenerative, cancer) as well as overall mortality risk. These findings suggest that
protein-level changes not only reflect biological aging but may predict health outcomes before
symptoms appear.

This project will reanalyze publicly available large-scale human proteomics datasets using a
quantitative MS/MS pipeline. The goal is to build an **interpretable machine learning model** that
predicts biological age from protein expression profiles. Special attention will be paid to
identifying which proteins drive the model's predictions, and examining their biological roles to
uncover new connections between molecular aging and disease pathways.

**Relevant skills and technologies:**

- Quantitative MS/MS proteomics analysis
- Python for bioinformatics workflows and machine learning
- Interpretable machine learning (e.g. SHAP, feature importance)
- Biological pathway interpretation

This project is available as a **master thesis** or as a **research internship** (levels 1 and 2).
